All figures are produced on a bid to bid basis, net of fund management charges with income reinvested.

For the purpose of this report, the term ‘Volatility’ is referring to standard deviation, this is a statistical measurement that, when applied to an investment fund, expresses its volatility, or risk. It shows how widely a range of returns varied from the fund’s average return over a particular period. Low volatility reduces the risk of buying into an investment in the upper range of its deviation cycle and then seeing its value head toward the lower extreme. FE measures volatility to one standard deviation, which means that 68% of returns will have fallen within that volatility band.
For example, if a fund had an average return of 5%, and its volatility was 15, this would mean that the range of its returns within one standard deviation had swung between +20% and -10%. Another fund with the same average return and 5% volatility would return between 10% and nothing, but there would be no loss in the majority of cases.

Life and Pension Funds: The yields quoted are for the Distribution Funds. They are calculated by dividing the distributions made by the funds in the past year by the fund's selling prices as at the month-end date you will find referenced on their factsheets.

Unit Trust/ISA Funds: The yields in the table are calculated as a percentage of the mid-market unit prices as at the month-end date referred to on the factsheets. Yields do not include any preliminary charge and investors may be subject to tax on distributions.

The distribution yields shown for the Corporate Bond Unit Trust, Investment Grade Corporate Bond Unit Trust, International Corporate Bond Unit Trust, Gilts Unit Trust, Index Linked Gilts Unit Trust and the Money Market Unit Trust are gross of tax and reflect the amounts that might be expected to be distributed over the next 12 months; they are based on a snapshot of the portfolios as at the month end referenced on their respective factsheets and for ISA investors gross units classes are available for these funds.

For the Money Market Unit Trust the underlying yield is equal to the distribution yield. For the Corporate Bond Unit Trust, Investment Grade Corporate Bond Unit Trust, International Corporate Bond Unit Trust and Gilts Unit Trust both the distribution yield and the underlying yield are shown.

The distribution yield is higher due to the impact of the expenses that are charged to capital. The yields on all other unit trusts are historic yields and reflect distributions over the past 12 months. Where funds have been launched within the last twelve months, we do not display a yield figure.

For the following unit trusts, a portion of the expenses is charged to capital. This has the effect of increasing the distributions for the year by 1.2% for the Corporate Bond Unit Trust, 1.0% for the Investment Grade Corporate Bond Unit Trust, 1.1% for the International Corporate Bond Unit Trust, 1.7% for the Equity Income Unit Trust, 1.7% for the Global Equity Income Unit Trust, 1.7% for the UK High Income Unit Trust, 1.6% for the Property Unit Trust, 1.5% for the Strategic Income Unit Trust, 1.2% for the Diversified Bond Unit Trust, 1.0% for the Index Linked Gilts Unit Trust, 1.9% for the Allshare Income Unit Trust, 1.9% for the UK & International Income Unit Trust, 0.7% for the Gilts (Class R) Unit Trust and 0.7% for the Gilts (Class L) Unit Trust, and constraining each fund’s capital performance to an equivalent extent.
