COMMENTARY

Investment markets have had a more balanced tone in July. Inflation has remained stubbornly high, exceeding expectations in the UK and America by ticking up to
9.4% and 8.6%, however this has been tempered by early signs that economies are slowing. Corporate earnings have generally been supportive, but at the same time, many companies have delivered guidance that tougher times may lie ahead.


This has allowed Central Bankers to subtly alter messaging, leaving investors hoping that the pace of interest rate rises will soon moderate, and any recession ahead may be shallow and brief. In this environment, the Ravenscroft Global Balanced Fund posted a return of 2.4% over the month of July.


No one knows in advance when a bottom is formed during an investment market correction. It is only after the event, when you are looking through your rear-view
mirror, that you realise the difficult days are behind you. This leads us to take several measured steps over time when we think we are getting close to a turning point, some will be anticipatory in nature, and some will be reactionary.


It is our view that the peak in inflation is close to hand, and we are therefore taking anticipatory action. Across the month we sold the 5% iShares USD TIPS holding and reinvested the proceeds into the iShares US Treasury 7-10 year (GBP hedged). This action will work to extend the average maturity of the portfolio’s fixed interest holdings, to make them more sensitive to a moderation in interest rate expectations. When we have confirmation that inflation has peaked, we will further extend the average maturity of the fixed interest exposures.


Finally, we believe the broad-based sell-off in investment markets we have experienced is throwing up pockets of opportunity. An area of particular interest to us is subordinated debt amongst financials, where the market pricing on these assets does not seem to reflect the balance sheet strengthening that has taken
place since the Global Financial Crisis.


Across the month we introduced an exposure to this area, via a 3% position in Sanlam Global Hybrid Capital Bond fund. The Fund is held elsewhere within our
strategies, so the portfolio, process and team are well known to us. The position will be funded from the remaining sale of the PIMCO Investment Grade Bond
fund. PIMCO have gone off the boil for a while as the macro-overlay which had reliably delivered excess returns for them over many years has been misfiring. This prompted a reduction in the holding earlier this year, and as subsequent conversations have not restored our confidence in their macro-overlay, it is the
catalyst for the sale of the remaining holding.
