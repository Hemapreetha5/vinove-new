COMMENTARY

[Test Commentary] Markets received something of a reprieve in October, led by resilient third quarter earnings, whilst measures announced to assist in easing the cost-of-living crisis in Europe also provided relief. Chinese equities were an exception, however, falling sharply in response to events related to the 20th Communist Party Conference, specifically President Xi’s reappointment to a third term as leader, and continued Covid restrictions. Developed market equities, as measured by the MSCI World Index, registered a gain of 7.2% over the month in USD terms, whereas the MSCI’s emerging market gauge fell -3.1%.
Large technology stocks aside, company earnings announcements in the US were mostly upbeat, broadly beating analysts’ expectations. Inflation remains elevated with consumer price index data (‘CPI’) showed prices rising at an annualised rate of 8.2%, though the reading came in lower than September’s print suggesting inflation may have peaked. Robust labour market data persists: the unemployment rate fell to an all-time low of 3.5%, prompting the Federal Reserve (‘Fed’) to continue tightening monetary conditions with a further 75 basis point (‘bps’) rate hike anticipated from the next Fed meeting at the beginning of November. US equities climbed in October; the S&P 500 was up 8.1%.
In Europe, the announcement of measures to cap energy bills, combined with unseasonably warm weather that saw gas prices sharply lower were a welcome boost to investor sentiment. Consumer prices increased 10.7% year-on-year, prompting the European Central Bank (‘ECB’) to hike rates by 75 bps at the ECB’s October meeting to 1.5% - the highest level since 2009. Purchasing Manager Indices (‘PMI’) data would suggest the Eurozone economy is heading for recession, the Composite PMI for September fell to 47.3 (below 50 constitutes contraction in business activity). The equity market, as measured by the MSCI Europe ex-UK Index, was up 6.6% in local currency terms.
In the UK, Rishi Sunak took over as Prime Minister with Jeremy Hunt as Chancellor and immediately reversed the policies introduced by Liz Truss and Kwasi Kwarteng in the preceding month. The U-turn was welcomed by investors, predominantly in fixed income and currency markets with yields falling sharply and Sterling appreciating markedly (+2.67% vs USD). Equities were also buoyed by the news, with the FTSE All Share Index returning 3.1% on a total return basis.
Perceived by investors as fiscally responsible, the market’s response to the reversal of proposed tax cuts caused Sterling-denominated fixed income assets to rally. Gains were seen across sovereign and credit markets, with UK Gilt indices returning 3.3% and Investment Grade corporates ending the month 5.2% higher. German Bunds and US Treasuries contracted however, down -0.4% and -1.4% respectively in anticipation of further central bank rate hikes with corresponding corporate returns +0.19% and -1.11%. Government Bond returns represented by the Bloomberg Barclays Government Bond Indices and corporate returns the ICE BofA Indices.
