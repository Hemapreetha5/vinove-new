Multi Asset - Growth

Fund Objective

The Fund is designed to grow well above inflation over the long-term by investing predominantly in a range of “growth-seeking” assets including shares, corporate bonds, infrastructure and property investments. Reflecting the Fund’s long term growth objective, the value of these assets can go down as well as up and falls in value can at times have a considerable impact on your total investments. From time to time the Fund may also invest a smaller proportion in what are considered to be more “defensive” assets such as investment grade corporate bonds, government bonds and cash to give some protection against very sharp falls in value. The Fund is intended to produce a long-term return of at least cash + 4% p.a., and this measure is shown as the “Benchmark” on this fund factsheet. The Fund is also measured against the returns generated by global equities (shown as the “Index Comparator”). These are expected to be higher over the long-term but with a greater risk of falls in value over shorter time periods.

Things you need to know

All funds carry some risk and you should consider these risks before making an investment decision. The value of investments and any income from them can fall as well as rise and will be reduced in real terms by the effects of inflation.

The main factors which may increase the risk of this fund include:

• The amount invested in company   shares
• The split between government and   corporate bonds
• The extent to which the fund invests in   high yield assets
• The amount held in overseas assets
• Any concentration in specific areas or   sectors

All DC funds in the Plan are ring-fenced from the Company, the investment managers and the investment platform provider. Each fund has its own legal and operational structure. Because the investment platform provider is not the fund manager, they “host” funds (managed by professional investment managers) in some instances through a long term insurance contract, instead of by a direct investment. To access these long term insurance contracts, a re-insurance arrangement is used between the platform provider and the investment manager. Reinsurance arrangements are not covered by the Financial Services Compensation Scheme. However, Scottish Widows require that all reinsurers must accept a deed of charge from Scottish Widows on their long term fund assets, as a layer of protection. Additionally, Scottish Widows rank equally with direct policy holders in the event of a problem. It is important to again remember that funds are ring-fenced, and the likelihood of an insolvency event is expected to be very low.

Further Information

Yearly Fund Charges - This figure includes the total charges and expenses that are deducted from the fund and are reflected in the unit price.

Cumulative Performance - Cumulative Performance shows the total growth of the fund over a specified period of time, expressed as a percentage of the value at the start of the period.

Annualised Performance - Annualised Performance shows the conversion of the growth of the fund over 3, 5 and 10 year periods into an equivalent annual rate, to aid comparison between different investments. The annual return is compounded over the time period being measured.

Rolling Year Performance - Rolling Year Performance shows the growth of the fund in percentage terms over each of the last five calendar years. The periods run up to the latest calendar quarter end (March, June, September and December).
