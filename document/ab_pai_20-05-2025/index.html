Integration: AB integrates material climate-related risks and opportunities into the security selection and portfolio construction processes for most actively managed strategies. The types of climate factors AB's analysts may evaluate, for the issuers in which we invest, include climate strategy and management, the political and regulatory backdrop, potential environmental liabilities, GHG emissions, low carbon technologies and climate opportunities.
Our climate approach is grounded in the development of deep research insights; elevated by engagement and stewardship with issuers; facilitated by climate-related data and tools; and draws on expertise from academic and industry partners, such as our relationship with the Columbia Climate School.
AB and Columbia University continue to collaborate in an ongoing effort to bridge the gap between finance and climate science and, in 2023, worked to better evaluate the physical risks posed by climate change, particularly in the US. Many sectors and assets are exposed to risks from natural disasters, particularly those intensified or catalysed by climate change. Through this partnership, AB and Columbia updated the NCDP’s US Natural Hazards Index, adding new data on hazards from extreme weather and natural disasters and on social vulnerability to enhance understanding.
To further strengthen how our investment teams manage climate risks and identify climate-related opportunities, AB has developed several proprietary research tools. In 2023 AB developed a Climate Transition Dashboard that allows our investment teams to easily view, compare and explore current climate change-related data on over 20,000 issuers across our actively managed equities and fixed income strategies. This tool pulls together data on emissions and emissions trajectories, implied temperature rises, green revenues, green bond issuance and other climate metrics for corporate issuers. Given the disparity and inconsistency of climate-related disclosures globally, the dashboard is a valuable tool that provides AB’s investment teams with a central place to access consistent climate data.
Engagement: In 2023 AB launched a proprietary Climate Transition Alignment Framework. This framework was designed to identify issuers driving heavily contributing to financed emissions in actively managed equities and corporate credit strategies and across more than 20 high-impact industries. High-impact industries are those that have particularly material risks and opportunities stemming from the transition to a lower-carbon economy. The framework is designed to identify specific areas of material risk and opportunities for these high-impact issuers and to signal to our investment teams where they should focus their research and engagement as we track issuers’ progress along the framework, when materially relevant for our clients.
In the 2023 calendar year, AB’s investment analysts conducted 585 engagements on the issue of carbon emissions and around 300 on climate transition plans.
Proxy Voting: In 2023, the volume of environmental shareholder proposals continued to rise, with a focus on enhanced reporting on GHG emissions. AB supports environmentally focused proposals that enhance shareholder value for our clients and promote genuine improvements in the management of the associated risks and opportunities for a business.

Integration: AB adheres to the UN Global Compact (“UNGC”) principles and the Organisation for Economic Cooperation and Development Guidelines for Multinational Enterprises (“OECD”). AB also monitors for UNGC breaches across our actively managed investment strategies. AB believes that the risk of breaching international norms is best understood and addressed through ongoing in-depth fundamental research, which enables us to analyse a wide range of information and properly assess the risk at an issuer-specific level.
As part of AB’s SFDR framework, AB uses compliance with the UNGC principles to determine whether a specific security can be deemed to promote environmental and/or social characteristics or comply with AB’s Good Governance Policy.
AB’s Good Governance Policy consists of security-specific analysis and scoring based on specific governance criteria, including sound management structures, employee relations, remuneration of staff and tax compliance, using a combination of external and internal data sources. The specific indicators used to assess compliance with these criteria include adherence to UNGC principles 3, 4, 5, 6 and 10. If at any point a security breaches the scoring threshold for any of the Good Governance indicators (including UNGC non-compliance with the specific principles listed above), then the security is deemed to fail the criteria for inclusion in AB’s Article 8 and 9 Products. For a security that breaches the criteria to continue to be held, or purchased, AB’s analysts must present research that refutes the findings of third-party data that has made the identification, and explain why the security remains a reasonable investment. This research will be subject to review by an independent internal committee.
Similarly, to determine if a security can be considered to promote environmental and/or characteristics, the issuer of the specific security must meet specific criteria, including compliance with all principles of the UNGC. Where a major third-party service provider has deemed an issuer to be in violation of international norms, our investment teams are required to research and document the nature of the breach, including, if applicable, why our internal research demonstrates that the security remains a reasonable investment. As per the Good Governance framework above, issuers that are deemed non-compliant with the UNGC may be considered to promote E/S characteristics if an AB’s analysts present research that refutes the findings of third-party data that has made the identification. This research will be subject to review by an independent internal committee. Our Responsibility team, in conjunction with our Risk division, has implemented a systematic process to ensure that this research is completed annually, as long as the position continues to be held. Engagement policiesAs part of the investment process for its actively managed portfolios, AB engages with issuers on a variety of topics for both gaining insights on the issuer and encouraging action that AB believes will benefit its clients as shareholders. More information on AB’s approach to engagement, including the most recent engagement reports, can be found here. More information on the AB Stewardship Approach can be found here.
Regarding engagement on ESG issues, AB encourages issuers to undertake actions that can help them better address material ESG risks or take advantage of ESG opportunities, in a way that meets our clients’ best interests.
AB uses engagement in two ways: insight and action.
• Insight: AB engages for additional insight into issuers and that insight is integrated into AB’s fundamental research on the investment. For example, through engagement AB gains a deeper understanding of an issuer’s renewable energy strategy and therefore more insight on the investment when AB is assessing alignment to environmental and/or social characteristics and any potential material financial impact.
• Action: AB engages to encourage issuers to take action that AB believes will enable them to better address material ESG risks or take advantage of ESG opportunities. For example, AB may encourage an issuer to consider a reduction of its environmental footprint, outlining the benefits of improved resilience to future regulation, carbon taxes and an enhanced ability to retain market share and talented employees. This active engagement helps promote environmental and social advancement, creates long-term issuer value, and maintains the investment alignment to environmental and social characteristics.
AB engages with issuers of securities held by respective AB Products but may also engage with issuers into which that AB Products have not yet, or will not, invest.
Engagements, which AB analysts and portfolio managers initiate directly with the issuer, may take place both before the initial investment is made and as part of AB’s ongoing monitoring. Engagements are often with senior executives, including an issuer’s CEO and CFO. During engagements, AB discusses strategy, business operations, governance, and a wide variety of other topics, including material ESG issues, with company management. Over time, analysts build a forum for open dialogue. AB also engages with directors, generally either to share its perspective on specific issues, or to escalate specific concerns after AB has engaged with management. In addition, AB engages with other stakeholders such as suppliers and customers as part of the process.
When AB prioritizes issuers to engage with, or themes and topics to discuss, several factors are considered, including the PAIs. AB’s analysts assess the ESG issue’s materiality, using the proprietary AB materiality map and industry-recognized frameworks. They also consider the company or issuer’s size and ESG ratings, AB’s historical proxy-voting record at the company, the size of AB’s Products exposure, the proportion of issuer assets held, the significance of AB’s security holding and the history and success of previous conversations.
AB may also conduct event-driven engagements when a company’s or issuer’s activity has had a significant negative impact, as well as company-led engagements when a firm reaches out to discuss an issue.
AB sometimes encounters situations where continued engagement is no longer productive or helpful in driving progress. In cases where AB deems the issuer’s behaviour to be misaligned with its clients’ best interests, it can escalate the engagement to communicate AB’s stance on key issues, conveying the gravity of our concern, and encourage the issuer to take action. AB may escalate an engagement in one or more of the following ways: proxy voting; sharing our views with other investors and/or stakeholders; publication of a public letter; writing a private letter to the board and management team; reducing a position or selling/not refinancing a security.
On a periodic basis, the relevant AB’ Investment Teams for the AB Products will create “Engagement Reports” that summarize issuer engagements and their outcomes. AB believes it is important to integrate material ESG Factors into ground-level fundamental research and documenting the engagement research and conclusions allows the AB Stewardship Approach to be successfully utilized.
References to international standardsIn the consideration of PAIs for AB Products, AB will use the following international standards for reference, where applicable:
• UN Global Compact, OECD Guidelines for Multinational Enterprises: AB adheres to the UN Global Compact (“UNGC”) principles and the Organisation for Economic Cooperation and Development Guidelines for Multinational Enterprises and AB monitors for UNGC breaches across our actively managed investment strategies.
• Paris Agreement: AB’s Products may consider an issuer’s decarbonisation strategy and asses its alignment with the long-term global warming objectives of the Paris Agreement. AB may assess whether the issuer has an externally validated decarbonisation pathway, has an external assessment of the alignment of its intensity profile with a global warming scenario of 2 degrees or, for financial institutions, is a signatory of the Net Zero Banking Alliance.
Please refer to section 1 for the PAI indicators used to measure alignment with the international standard and to section 2 for the methodology and data used.
