AIMS AND OBJECTIVES

The Global Blue Chip Fund’s main objective is to generate long-term capital growth from a focused portfolio of large global businesses, which are listed on recognized stock exchanges in developed countries. It is actively managed and not constrained by a benchmark. Ravenscroft Global Blue Chip is a sub fund of the Ravenscroft Investment Fund Offshore; an open-ended, multiclass Unit Trust authorized as a Class B Scheme by the Guernsey Financial Services Commission.

COMMENTARY

November witnessed a sharp rebound in risk assets as investors grew in confidence that the current rate rising cycle is all but done. Markets rebounded hard from technically oversold levels after October’s dismal performance and remained robust through to the end of the month. Your fund returned 4.92% whilst the broader market returned 4.84%.
Unsurprisingly, risk-on sectors such as Discretionary, Technology, and Communication Services contributed the most to our overall performance. Traditional defensive assets such as Healthcare lagged, and our Staples positions generated a negative return overall. The risk-on appetite saw some of our most beaten-up stocks rally the hardest with on-line retailer Etsy leading the charge. The stock returned over 16% in sterling terms contributing over 50 basis points to performance, making it our top contributor also. Diageo offset some of this after it announced a sharp decline in profitability within its Latin America region due to excess inventories and downtrading by consumers who are feeling the pinch. This resulted in the stock downgrading growth expectations for next year.
Of particular note was the performance of new entrant to the portfolio Estee Lauder, a global leader in prestige beauty, which we introduced after shares had undergone a period of significant de-rating. The precipitous share price decline had been associated with inventory challenges within its Asia Travel Retail business. Whilst it is early days, we suspect these issues to be temporary in nature and we anticipate that Estēe Lauder will prove to be another example of where our willingness to look through short-term issues and focus on the longer-term opportunities will benefit our investors. In her autobiography, Estēe commented that ““women have always enhanced their god given looks. It has always been so. It will always be so.” Our investment in the business is a bet on the persistence of this trend and the desirability of the many fantastic brands it sells through its enviable global distribution network.
Our investment in Estee encapsulates the concept of time-arbitrage - the greatest edge inherent within our process. We believe markets generally underappreciate the true value of great businesses over the long-term. When short-term issues exacerbate the mispricing further a compelling investment opportunity arises. We intend to explain more on both the opportunity with Estee and the concept of time arbitrage in our next quarterly newsletter.
