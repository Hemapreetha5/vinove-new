Portfolio Activity & Performance  

Fund Summary

Management continues to execute on the fund strategy, with key highlights for the June 2025 quarter are:

  • Successful leasing of 1,340 sqm at SunshinePrivate Hospital for 15 years, with the option (for the tenant) to expend thisby a further 1,460 sqms in the future.

  • Portfolio underpinned by Weighted AverageLease Expiry (WALE) of 14.98 years and occupancy of 94.9%.

  • Divestment of a further 3 non-core assetstotalling $11.4 million and have also entered into sale arrangements to divestproperties totalling $132.3 million.

  • Successfulrenewal of debt facility agreement totalling $325 million.

  • Greenstar Portfolio rating completedin July 2025 for first tranche of 21 HPT assets.

On 11 July 2025, the Lease to Sunshine Private Pty Ltd (in Liquidation) was surrendered by the Receiver and Manager, effective the same day. On 18 July 2025, EY retired as Receiver and Manager.

The former hospital operator had two primary uses: Mental Health services located on Level 3, and Surgical services (including overnight beds and operating theatres) located on Level 1. 

We are pleased to advise that a new operator, Sunshine Clinic HoldCo Pty Ltd, has entered into a binding lease agreement for a 15-year term commencing 1 September 2025. The lease covers the existing 21 Mental Health beds on Level 3 and a portion of Level 2, which will be repurposed as a day program facility (~1,339.6sqm). The executed lease has improved the occupancy of the property from 25.62% to 33.94%. The lease includes rights to expand into the balance of Level 3 bedrooms, previously used for surgical services, on a staged basis (~1,430.1sqm). 

The Trust’s rental income is underpinned by high quality tenant covenant and the top 10 tenants are ASX-listed, Government, national and multi-national operators. Given the ongoing media attention on the position of the Healthscope hospital portfolio, we would like to reassure investors that the Trust has no exposure to Healthscope. When a portfolio of 11 Healthscope properties was made available for sale in 2022, our analysis found that acquisition of that portfolio would not be accretive to investor returns or otherwise fit with the Trust’s portfolio requirements. The Trust is a long-term owner of hospital and other healthcare assets and continues to invest in critical community infrastructure that serves the healthcare needs of Australians.

Property Valuations

During the June 2025 quarter, the Trust revalued 33 properties, which resulted in:

  • a marginal reduction invaluation of 1.59% and the cap rate softened from 5.41% to 5.42%;

Outlook

In a similar vein to the March 2025 quarter, Management continues to remain focused on improving the Trust’s Balance Sheet through a number of capital management initiatives (predominantly the divestment of non-core assets), with the goal of returning the Trust back to the top of its target gearing range towards mid- FY26.

Despite some persistent volatility over the quarter (as a result of significant global events), we remain cautiously optimistic that the Australian property market will continue to show signs of the recovery (seen through late FY25). Over the quarter, total transaction volume (across most real-estate asset classes) continued to increase and we expect that additional of demographic and structural tail winds will further support valuations for Australian health care real-estate. 

Market Commentary 

Continuing with the themes from the first half of CY2025, significant global events such as escalating conflict across the Middle East as well as tariff [and energy] tensions continued to dominate markets globally.

As a result, central banks have recently taken a more hawkish posture with the US Fed re-affirming a “wait and see” stance for their July-25 meeting, whilst domestically, the RBA controversially held the cash rate (at 3.85%) in their July 2025 policy meeting. Despite this, market participants are still projecting the RBA to cut at least 2 more times this Calander Year.

Domestically over the quarter, inflation reduced to 2.1% in May 2025 (from 2.4% in April 2025), whilst real GDP grew 1.3% YoY and household consumption improved by a moderate 0.4% (predominantly due to stronger expenditure on food and electricity). Private investment also increased, growing 0.7% over the quarter with residential housing markets (particularly across major cities) showing signs of a pricing rebound. 

Looking forward, as we move into second half of CY2025, commercial property markets continue to show signs of improvement with transactions volumes increasing across most asset classes. Over the first half of CY2025, total transaction volumes increased 13.0% YoY ($15.5 billion), due largely to domestic inflationary trends and increased offshore investment. Industrial property led the way increasing 98% YoY, with retail up 29% YoY, commercial office up 11% YoY.

The outlook Healthcare property performance also improved over the quarter, as seen in the March 2025 MSCI Total Return index, Healthcare property (on a total return basis) trended back into positive territory, in line with other commercial real estate asset classes.

From the operators perspective, the private hospital sector continues to play a pivotal role in the Australian healthcare landscape, accounting for over 40% of hospital admissions and a substantial 70% of all elective surgeries. This level of contribution underscores its centrality not only to the system’s financial sustainability but also to the effective functioning of national hospital services.

Private health insurance uptake with hospital cover has shown modest growth, rising from 45.1% in June 2018 to 45.3% by the end of March 2025. While incremental, this uptick reflects ongoing consumer reliance on private health options to supplement public services.

The impact of the COVID-19 pandemic served to reinforce and hasten a broader international trend: a marked shift toward shorter hospital stays. This transition has been enabled by technological advancements in surgery, along with greater adoption of Telehealth and sophisticated remote monitoring. These innovations are reshaping the patient experience and hospital resource allocation.

It's important to delineate the scope of care between facility types. Day and short-stay hospitals are increasingly suitable for sub-acute procedures, offering efficiency and faster recovery times. However, acute interventions—particularly cardiac and neurological operations—remain the domain of long-stay hospitals due to their complexity and associated risks.

According to Queensland’s hospital rescue plan, one of the solutions to reduce waitlist is to utilise private sector capacity to deliver planned elective surgery within private hospitals.
