Fund Objective
To provide capital growth through investment in collective investment schemes. These collective investment schemes will provide exposure to shares with the remainder providing exposure to a mix of asset classes (including, but not limited to, fixed interest securities and property assets) and absolute return strategies. • Investing in equities generally has the potential for higher capital growth over the longer term than investing in, say, fixed interest securities. However there might be considerable fluctuations in equity prices and there is a greater risk that you might not get all your money back.

• Exchange rate changes might cause the value of any overseas investments to go up or down.

• Property is a less liquid asset than other assets such as bonds or equities and values could be affected if properties need to be sold in a short timescale. Property valuation is generally a matter of judgement by an independent valuer rather than fact and values can go up or down.

• Some of the companies and governments who issue the bonds that the funds invest in might not be able to meet their payments, or their credit rating might fall. If they don’t meet their payments, or their credit rating falls, the value of your investment might reduce. In addition, a proportion of the Fund can be invested in high yield bonds (a type of fixed interest security with a low rating from a credit agency), these can offer a higher income than bonds with a high credit rating but carry a higher risk of not being able to meet their payments.

• Fluctuations in interest rates are likely to affect the value of the bonds held by the funds. If longterm interest rates rise, the value of your shares is likely to fall and vice versa.

Quarterly Fund Manager Review

Equity markets gained in the quarter with the IT sector outperforming amid enthusiasm around artificial intelligence. Major central banks continued to raise interest rates, although the Federal Reserve (Fed) paused in June. Government bond yields were on the rise again despite some divergence, with the UK underperforming due to higher-than-expected inflation and a greater resolve by the central bank to combat inflation. The fund started the quarter with long equity position but moved short early on so that it was positioned for a slowdown in economic activity. This short was held in S&P, Eurostoxx, Nikkei and FTSE. It was implemented to reflect tighter credit conditions with higher rates causing strain across the global economy, all the while higher inflation was forcing both the Fed and the ECB to maintain tighter monetary policy in the near term. With positioning moving to a slowdown, a long European and US small caps positions was implemented to act as a contrarian upside risk hedge in the context of being short equities and long bonds. The US 10 year bond future was added during May as an expression of our positive view on duration. As inflation continued to fall and we saw labour market conditions peak, we believed the Federal Reserve’s hawkish tone was likely to shift to a more balanced assessment in the next few months. The US high yield position was closed during the quarter as our view moved to neutral. A long Australian dollar versus Canadian dollar position as a means of expressing a mean reversion opportunity between two markets that are exposed to similar reaction functions by the local Central bank. A tactical long Norwegian krona versus the Swedish krone currency trade was introduced, partly as a mean reversion trade and partly to pickup a rebound in the oil price which Norway is more sensitive to. This was closed with a profit. The fund continued to hold the small commodities position first introduced in 2022 as a hedge against inflation and positive growth surprises.
