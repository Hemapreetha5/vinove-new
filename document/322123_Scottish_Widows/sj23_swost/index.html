Quarterly Fund Manager Review

UK equities declined over the second quarter as the Bank of England (BoE) continued to tighten monetary policy amid concerning levels of inflation. To the dismay of the BoE, UK headline inflation was stagnant in May at 8.7%, which was above forecasts and remained the highest in major developed markets. Meanwhile, the core inflation reading, which excludes food and energy, rose to 7.1%. In economic data, there were some signs of improvement, but this also threatened to add to inflationary pressures. Wage growth came in hot, at 6.5% in April, and consumer confidence rose for the fifth straight month in June. The CBI’s indicator of manufacturing optimism also rose, as did the UK composite PMI, which showed an increase in services sector activity. With a combination of stubborn inflation and some strengthening in the economy – wage growth’s potential to bake in higher inflation particularly concerning – the BoE raised interest rates twice over the quarter, by 25 basis points (bps) in May and by 50bps in June, taking the policy rate to 5.0%. This surprised the market as other major central banks had been reducing the magnitude/pace of rate rises. With forecasts UK rates could reach over 6.0%, expectations of higher rates for longer were priced in over the quarter, which pushed up bond yields, ten-year gilt yields soaring to levels last seen as a result of the infamous ‘mini budget’ of autumn 2022. The rising rate backdrop contributed to the strengthening of sterling against the US dollar, which negatively impacted companies with USD sales and further weighed on the natural resources sector. The UK market’s relatively high weighting in natural resources was also a drag as commodity price movements were weak and the global equity rally continued to be led by mega-cap technology companies. Rising rate expectations also caused swap rates, which lenders use to price fixed rate mortgages, to rise; this depressed valuations of some areas of the domestic market such as home builders. The FTSE 100 index and the more domestic stocks in the FTSE All-Share index both declined over the quarter.
